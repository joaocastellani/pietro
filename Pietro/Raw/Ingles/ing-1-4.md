### 7.3 — Focus on the language — Modals of advice and obligation

**Nome das estruturas:** Modals of advice and obligation (Modais de conselho e obrigação)

---

#### Should / Shouldn't — Conselho / Recomendação

**Uso:** Para dar conselhos e recomendações. Indica que algo é uma boa ideia, mas não é obrigatório.

**Estrutura:** Subject + **should/shouldn't** + infinitive (sem *to*)

**Exemplos:**
- *"You **should** talk to a trusted person about how you feel."*
- *"You **shouldn't** keep your emotions bottled up."*
- *"I think you **should** consider speaking with a school counselor."* [do diálogo]

---

#### Must — Obrigação forte (interna)

**Uso:** Obrigação forte — sentimento pessoal de necessidade ou regra absoluta. Frequentemente vem de dentro do próprio sujeito.

**Estrutura:** Subject + **must** + infinitive (sem *to*)

**Exemplos:**
- *"I **must** find a way to manage my emotions."*
- *"Students **must** not share personal information online."*

---

#### Have to — Obrigação (externa)

**Uso:** Obrigação imposta por uma regra ou circunstância externa — não vem de dentro do sujeito.

**Estrutura:** Subject + **have to / has to** + infinitive (sem *to*)

**Exemplos:**
- *"Students **have to** follow the school's online forum rules."*
- *"You **don't have to** visit each country — it's a recommendation, not a rule."*

---

#### May / Might — Possibilidade

**Uso:** Indicar possibilidade. *May* é ligeiramente mais formal que *might*.

**Exemplos:**
- *"You **may find** it helps to write about your emotions."* [do texto]
- *"You **might** want to start small — write down what you feel."* [do texto]

---

#### Why don't you — Sugestão

**Uso:** Forma de sugestão informal e direta.

**Estrutura:** **Why don't you** + infinitive (sem *to*)

**Exemplo:**
- *"**Why don't you** talk to your school counselor?"*

## 11. FLASHCARDS DO CAPÍTULO

**FC-1**
🔵 Front: What is **should/shouldn't** used for?
🟢 Back: To give **advice or recommendations** — it's a good idea, but not obligatory. Structure: subject + should/shouldn't + infinitive. Ex: *"You should talk to someone you trust."* / *"You shouldn't keep your feelings hidden."*

**FC-2**
🔵 Front: What is **must** used for? How is it different from **have to**?
🟢 Back: **Must** = strong obligation from the speaker's own feelings or conviction (internal). **Have to** = obligation from an external rule or circumstance. Ex: *"I must control my emotions"* (my own conviction) vs. *"Students have to follow the rules"* (external rule).

**FC-3**
🔵 Front: What is **have to** used for?
🟢 Back: To express **external obligation** — a rule, requirement or circumstance that forces the action. Structure: subject + have to/has to + infinitive. Ex: *"You have to see a doctor if this continues."* Negative: *"You don't have to come"* = it's not necessary (NOT that it's forbidden).

**FC-4**
🔵 Front: What is **may / might** used for?
🟢 Back: To express **possibility**. *May* is slightly more formal. *Might* suggests a lower degree of probability. Ex: *"You may find it helps to write."* / *"You might want to start small."* Both from the Reading text.

**FC-5**
🔵 Front: What is **why don't you** used for?
🟢 Back: An informal way to make a **suggestion**. Structure: *Why don't you + infinitive*. Ex: *"Why don't you talk to your counselor?"* It's softer than *should* and more direct than *might*.

**FC-6**
🔵 Front: What does **anxious** mean? How is it different from **nervous**?
🟢 Back: **Anxious** = worried about something uncertain; a deeper, longer-lasting unease. **Nervous** = feeling/showing anticipation or fear about something specific and imminent. Both describe discomfort, but *anxious* is more about the unknown; *nervous* is about a specific upcoming event.

**FC-7**
🔵 Front: What does **frustrated** mean?
🟢 Back: Feeling unhappy or annoyed because something didn't work out as expected; doubting oneself. Ex: *"I feel frustrated when I can't express my feelings out loud."*

**FC-8**
🔵 Front: What is an **online forum post**?
🟢 Back: A type of text published on an internet forum where users share questions, experiences or opinions and invite responses. It is written in first person, personal but respectful, and must protect the user's privacy. It's the writing genre of this chapter.
