### 2.2 — Reading text

- **Título:** Do you know what the Matilda Effect is?
- **Autor:** não citado
- **Fonte:** Based on: https://www.nomorematildas.com/ and https://www.lostwomenofscience.org/news/the-matilda-effect-how-women-are-becoming-invisible-in-science. Accessed on: Jul. 11, 2024.
- **Gênero:** article
- **Texto integral:**

The Matilda Effect is named to honor American activist and sociologist Matilda Joslyn Gage (1826-1898), a woman who fought for equal rights and spoke about the unfair treatment of women in science. She noticed that many women scientists did amazing discoveries, but their research was ignored or denied credit or, in some cases, given to men instead. A classic example of a female scientist whose discoveries were attributed to a man is Austrian physicist Lise Meitner. She had to work harder for her position in science than her male colleagues. One of them was Otto Han, winner of the Nobel Prize, for the discovery of nuclear fission. Analyzing their correspondence at that time, it's clear, Otto Han understood little about physics. She was never honored. Another example is Rosalind Franklin, whose scientific findings were essential to identify the DNA structure, but the credit of her work was given to her colleagues James Watson and Francis Crick.

Because of the Matilda Effect, girls might think that science is only for boys, which is not true. Many women have made big contributions to science in the past, but their names were forgotten.

Today, groups like #NoMoreMatildas want to bring attention to more women scientists. These groups are fighting to put female scientists' names, stories and discoveries in textbooks, so girls can be inspired by them and know that STEM has no gender.

**Estrutura 2: Modal verbs**

Now, let's review the use of some **modal verbs**:

| Modal verb | Use |
|---|---|
| **Have to** | used for obligation |
| **Must** | refers to rules |
| **May** | refers to possible actions or request and permission |
| **Might** | used for possibility (weaker than "*may*") |
| **Should** | used for advice or recommendation |

---

**Estrutura 3: Conditional sentences**

Do you remember how to use and structure **conditional sentences**?

- **First conditional**: used for real or possible situations in the future. It is formed by *If + Simple present + will/won't + infinitive*.
  - Example: *If my parents **give** me money for my birthday, I **will save** it to buy a new video game.*

- **Second conditional**: refers to hypothetical or unlikely situations and their probable consequences. The second conditional is formed by *If + Simple past + would/wouldn't + infinitive*.
  - Example: *If I **had** the chance to interview Zendaya, I **would ask** her about the new movie.*

## 5. WRITING TIME

### 5.2 — Focus on the genre

- **Nome do gênero:** Campaign
- **Características conforme o material:** Campaigns aim to advertise products, services or, as in the case of the text, a cause. These texts are concise and objective, because their messages must be easily understood by their audience so people buy products or services advertised or take action to support their cause. To call the readers' attention, these texts use informal language, including hashtags, in the case of social media posts, and usually have visual aids, such as photos, illustrations and graphs.

### 5.3 — Writing task

Still in pairs, follow the instructions to create your campaign.

— Think of an issue you want to address in your campaign.

— Decide where you want to promote it. Are you going to create a poster and stick it in school hallways? Will you post it on your social media or on your school's website?

— Create a short hashtag to summarize why you think this issue is important and write a simple sentence to explain it.

— Choose an image related to the issue to illustrate your poster/post and draw your audience's attention to it.

— Write a short paragraph explaining what your campaign is about and how you plan to put it into action. Make your text engaging to capture the readers' attention and effectively promote your cause.

— Share your campaign with your teacher and ask for feedback. Make any necessary changes or corrections.

— Prepare the final version of your poster/post.

— Share your work with your class and then with the whole school. Try to put your ideas into practice.

## 7. FLASHCARDS DO CAPÍTULO

**FC-1**
🔵 Front: What does "look up to" mean in English?
🟢 Back: To admire someone. Ex: *I really look up to Mary.* (Eu realmente admiro a Mary.)

**FC-2**
🔵 Front: What is a "role model"?
🟢 Back: A person someone admires and uses as an example of good behavior or achievement. Ex: *She is a role model to me.* (Ela é um modelo para mim.)

**FC-3**
🔵 Front: What is the Matilda Effect?
🟢 Back: A phenomenon in which women's work in science is ignored or credited to men instead of the women who actually did the research.

**FC-4**
🔵 Front: What linking word expresses contrast?
🟢 Back: *but* or *however*. Ex: *I want to participate in the debate. However, I don't have time.* (Contraste / Contra-argumento)

**FC-5**
🔵 Front: What linking words express addition?
🟢 Back: *and*, *also*, *besides*. Ex: *Besides the president, I also admire that congresswoman.* (Além disso / Adição)

**FC-6**
🔵 Front: What is the difference between "must" and "have to"?
🟢 Back: **Must** refers to rules. **Have to** expresses obligation. Ex: *You must use a pen.* (regra) / *She has to participate.* (obrigação)

**FC-7**
🔵 Front: Which modal verb expresses possibility weaker than "may"?
🟢 Back: **Might**. Ex: *Their disagreement might cause trouble.* (Pode ser que cause problemas — possibilidade mais fraca.)

**FC-8**
🔵 Front: How is the First Conditional formed?
🟢 Back: *If + Simple present + will/won't + infinitive*. Ex: *If my parents give me money, I will save it.* (Situação real ou possível no futuro.)

**FC-9**
🔵 Front: How is the Second Conditional formed?
🟢 Back: *If + Simple past + would/wouldn't + infinitive*. Ex: *If I had the chance, I would ask her about the movie.* (Situação hipotética ou improvável.)

**FC-10**
🔵 Front: What does "thoughtful" mean?
🟢 Back: Caring and worried about the well-being of other people. (Atencioso/a, que pensa no bem-estar dos outros.)

**FC-11**
🔵 Front: What does "good-hearted" mean?
🟢 Back: Someone who is kind and tries to help others before or without them asking for it. (De bom coração, bondoso/a.)

**FC-12**
🔵 Front: What is a "campaign" as a genre?
🟢 Back: A concise and objective text that aims to advertise products, services or a cause. It uses informal language, hashtags and visual aids to call readers' attention and promote action.

**FC-13**
🔵 Front: What does "may" express in grammar?
🟢 Back: Possible actions or request and permission. Ex: *May I go to the restroom, please?* (Posso ir ao banheiro, por favor?)

**FC-14**
🔵 Front: What does "should" express?
🟢 Back: Advice or recommendation. Ex: *I think you should talk to your parents.* (Acho que você deveria conversar com seus pais.)

**FC-15**
🔵 Front: What linking words express condition?
🟢 Back: *if* and *unless*. Ex: *I won't talk to Paul unless he apologizes.* (*Unless* = if not / a menos que.)
