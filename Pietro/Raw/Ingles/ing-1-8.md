### 2.2 — Reading text

- **Título:** Help your teenager manage their money for life
- **Autor:** não citado
- **Fonte:** Based on: Ages 13 to 17: Teaching teens to budget. *MoneySense*, c2024. Available at: https://www.moneysense.ca/learn/teaching-kids-about-money-ages-13-to-17/. Accessed on: Aug. 29, 2024.
- **Gênero:** article
- **Texto integral:**

As kids become teenagers, it's important to help them think of money in a smarter way. Instead of just thinking about small amounts of money they get each week, their allowance, they should think about bigger amounts over a month. *"Teens should start receiving more money less often, and they need to begin taking care of some of their own spending,"* says Certified Financial Planner Trevor Van Nest. For example, if your teenager usually gets an allowance of $20 weekly, you might switch to giving them $80 a month, plus a bit extra for things like music subscriptions, clothes, shoes, movies etc. They should be encouraged to manage their money, plan and save it to buy what they want in the future. If they want to purchase a new cell phone, they should look for the price and plan how much money they will need to save monthly to buy it.

Many teenagers start getting part-time jobs during this time. Whether it's working at a local store in the summer or a few shifts a week somewhere else in town, you can help by teaching them things as minimum wage and how to balance work and school. Most teens will also be curious about why they don't get to keep all the money they earn because of taxes deduction. They might be surprised to learn that earning $10 an hour doesn't mean they will keep $10 an hour.

When they start earning their own money, it's a clever idea to talk to them about saving money to achieve bigger dreams. If they have a dream of traveling abroad, for example, they can start planning and saving money for it. Encourage them to set aside some money for future plans and dreams.

### 3.2 — Take a tip!

**Take a tip! (Vocabulary Time, pág. 118)**
Para nos referirmos a dinheiro em cédulas, dizemos *banknotes*, e em moedas, *coins*.

**Take a tip! (Vocabulary Time, pág. 119)**
Assim como em português, em inglês temos frases em sentido figurado que são utilizadas em contextos informais. Elas são chamadas **expressões idiomáticas** (*idiomatic expressions*). Em alguns casos, observamos uma correspondência direta entre uma expressão idiomática em língua portuguesa e em língua inglesa, como em *"Break the ice."* ("Quebrar o gelo."). Contudo, isso não acontece na maior parte das frases; por isso, é importante compreender o contexto em que elas são empregadas.

**Take a tip! (Connecting with Financial education, pág. 125)**
A expressão *"to pay in cash"* pode significar "pagar em dinheiro" ou "pagar à vista". Embora no Brasil a prática de parcelar as compras seja comum há muitos anos, isso não ocorre em outros países. A modalidade de pagamento parcelado nos Estados Unidos, por exemplo, se tornou mais popular após a crise econômica gerada pela pandemia de covid-19, em 2020.

| Modal verb | Function |
|---|---|
| **must** | Rules |

| **may** | Possible actions or permission |
| **might** | Weaker possibility compared to *may* |
| **should** | Advice |
| **mustn't** | Prohibition |

---

**Estrutura 2: Mustn't vs. Don't/Doesn't have to**

Do you remember the difference between **mustn't** and **don't/doesn't have to**?

**Mustn't** refers to something that is forbidden, while **don't/doesn't have to** implies something that is unnecessary.

- *You **mustn't** cross the street on a red signal.* (If you do it, you may get fined or injured.)
- *You **don't have to** do your homework today. It's due two weeks from now.* (You can do it tomorrow or another day, not necessarily now.)

---

**Focus on the genre — Opinion articles**

**Opinion articles** are very common in websites, magazines and newspapers, in which the author presents and defends their ideas and points of view about a current topic. The language is formal and used to persuade the reader. Although this kind of text has facts, it carries the personal view of its author.

## 7. FLASHCARDS DO CAPÍTULO

**FC-1**
🔵 Front: What does "mustn't" express?
🟢 Back: Prohibition — something that is forbidden. Ex: *"You mustn't cross the street on a red signal."* (Você não deve / é proibido atravessar no sinal vermelho.)

**FC-2**
🔵 Front: What is the difference between "mustn't" and "don't have to"?
🟢 Back: **Mustn't** = forbidden (proibição). **Don't have to** = unnecessary (desnecessário, mas permitido). Ex: *"You mustn't speed."* (proibido) / *"You don't have to wear a tie."* (não é necessário, mas pode.)

**FC-3**
🔵 Front: What does "should" express?
🟢 Back: Advice or recommendation. Ex: *"You should save money for the future."* (Você deveria guardar dinheiro para o futuro.)

**FC-4**
🔵 Front: What does "have to" express?
🟢 Back: Obligation or necessity. Ex: *"She has to pay her bills."* (Ela é obrigada a pagar suas contas.)

**FC-5**
🔵 Front: What does "might" express?
🟢 Back: Weaker possibility compared to *may*. Ex: *"It might rain today."* (Talvez chova hoje — menos certeza que *may*.)

**FC-6**
🔵 Front: What does "save for a rainy day" mean?
🟢 Back: To save money for an emergency. (Guardar dinheiro para emergências.) Ex: *"I decided to save my bonus for a rainy day."*

**FC-7**
🔵 Front: What does "break the bank" mean?
🟢 Back: To be extremely expensive or spend all the money at once. (Custar muito caro / gastar tudo de uma vez.) Ex: *"He tried to buy a phone that wouldn't break the bank."*

**FC-8**
🔵 Front: What does "bring home the bacon" mean?
🟢 Back: To earn the money that is needed to live. (Ser o provedor / ganhar o sustento.) Ex: *"I raised my kid on my own. I had to bring home the bacon."*

**FC-9**
🔵 Front: What does "look like a million bucks" mean?
🟢 Back: Looking really good or being in a good mood. (Estar com ótima aparência / radiante.) Ex: *"Wow, you look like a million bucks in this haircut!"*

**FC-10**
🔵 Front: What is an "opinion article" as a genre?
🟢 Back: A text common in websites, magazines and newspapers in which the author presents and defends their ideas about a current topic. The language is formal and persuasive, though it carries the personal view of its author.

**FC-11**
🔵 Front: What is the difference between "wants" and "needs"?
🟢 Back: **Wants** = things you desire but can live without (ex: designer clothes, a new phone). **Needs** = things essential for survival (ex: food, water, shelter).

**FC-12**
🔵 Front: What does "allowance" mean?
🟢 Back: Mesada. Ex: *"Do you get an allowance from your family?"* (Você recebe mesada da sua família?)

**FC-13**
🔵 Front: What does "budget" mean in the context of personal finance?
🟢 Back: Planejar-se financeiramente. Ex: *"Budgeting is an important life skill to learn."* (Planejar-se financeiramente é uma habilidade importante.)

**FC-14**
🔵 Front: What is BNPL?
🟢 Back: Buy Now, Pay Later — a payment method in which you buy something and pay for it in installments over time. Ex: Buying a phone for $1,000 and paying in four installments of $250 each.

**FC-15**
🔵 Front: What does "mustn't" mean in Portuguese?
🟢 Back: É proibido / não pode. É um modal de **proibição**. Ex: *"You mustn't exceed the speed limit."* (É proibido ultrapassar o limite de velocidade.)
